# Personas: A Specialist You Shaped

A persona is not a character voice pasted into a prompt. It is a useful role with a clear body of context and a repeatable method.

Take a research assistant. Its role might include finding primary sources, separating evidence from interpretation, and producing a short memo with links. The persona becomes credible when those expectations are visible: the knowledge base it may use, [[skills-teach-ai-how|the skill it follows]], and the questions it must escalate.

That makes personas practical to improve. If the research assistant is too quick to trust a secondary source, you do not need to hope for a better answer next time. You can change the review rule. If it is missing context, you can add a source to the knowledge base. The expertise is inspectable.

This is also why a persona should stay narrow at first. “Be my perfect business partner” gives an agent a flattering title and no workable assignment. “Prepare a source-backed briefing on this question, using these sources and this checklist” gives it a role it can actually perform.

As you build more skills and better context, a small set of roles can become genuinely useful. They do not replace your judgment. They give [[05-leverage-in-practice/what-the-human-still-owns|the part you still own]] a repeatable way to show up in the work.
